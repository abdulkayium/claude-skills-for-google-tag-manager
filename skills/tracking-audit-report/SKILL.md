# Tracking Audit Report

Makes technical findings easy to read for an owner or a marketing lead.

## Use this when
- An audit is done and the findings need to go to a client.
- A manager wants a one-page view of the problems.

## Inputs to ask for
1. The raw findings (notes, screenshots, outputs from other skills).
2. The audience (owner, marketer, developer).
3. The business goal and main platforms.
4. Any numbers that are checked and safe to share.

## Workflow
1. Write the **summary** in 3 to 4 lines: what is fine, what is not, what it costs the business, what to do first.
2. Turn every finding into one row: issue, why it matters, evidence, fix, effort, priority.
3. Rank by business impact (lost or wrong data for money decisions first).
4. Pull out **quick wins**: fixes under one hour or one day.
5. Add a **what is working** list. It builds trust and shows what was checked.
6. Add **next steps** with an owner and a rough order.
7. Remove jargon. If a technical word is needed, add a plain explanation next to it.

## Output format
1. Summary
2. Priority findings (table)
3. Quick wins
4. What is working
5. Next steps
6. What we need from you

## Decision rules
- Use high, medium and low priority. Do not invent a score.
- Every claim needs evidence from the findings. If evidence is missing, mark it "to confirm".
- Keep it to two pages of reading time. Move details to an appendix.

## Guardrails
- Writing only. Do not send the report or change any system. Give the text for the user to review and send.
- Do not invent numbers, losses or percentages. Use only what is in the findings.
- Do not name other clients. Do not include secrets, logins or personal data.
- Do not promise results. Describe the risk and the fix.
